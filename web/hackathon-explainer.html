A local energy mismatch
The challenge

Solar supply and local demand don’t always line up.

One household can export solar while another has demand or a battery to charge. The electricity still moves through the existing network. The coordination question is whether a transparent plan can better align when local renewable supply and flexible demand are available.

A neighbour’s home load or battery could use energy in the same interval, subject to power and transfer limits.

What SunShift is testing: can interval-level planning make a local match easier to inspect than a single “trade amount” number? The prototype models this; it does not send electricity, place a market bid or settle a payment.

Why this approach

Not a claims dashboard.
A constrained plan.

The prototype’s contribution is making the assumptions and limits part of the plan rather than hiding them behind a headline number.

01

Match by interval, not just by monthly total

The planner allocates solar to buyer demand in hourly slices and keeps the transfer-cap constraint visible.

02

Include flexible demand

A fixed storage-energy task is scheduled within a charging window and power limit, so the comparison includes demand coordination.

03

Make scenario prices auditable

Peer and grid reference prices are explicit user inputs. Cost figures are modeled and can change when those assumptions change.

04

Bind the plan to a local receipt

The local SHA-256 chain detects edits and duplicate plan claims. A Solidity registry reference exists separately, but it is not deployed or connected.

Build status

What is working now—and what remains a research step.

Working prototype

Hourly optimizer

Validates inputs, preserves flexible-energy and power limits, prioritizes solar surplus and matches under the configured transfer cap.

Working prototype

Forecast + profile inputs

Loads an offline example, optionally requests Open-Meteo weather, or accepts a user CSV. User-supplied profiles are not independently verified.

Working prototype

Local evidence record

A tested append-only hash chain verifies its entries and rejects duplicate event IDs or plan hashes. It is not public blockchain infrastructure.

Not implemented yet

No smart-meter feed, household control, live NEM dispatch, retailer settlement, payment, deployed smart contract or proof of physical delivery.

Next validation

Use consented interval-meter data, a real tariff/settlement model, network-loss assumptions, participant feedback and a testnet integration before claiming real savings or market readiness.
